# Interview Protocol v1.0 – ProcuraCost Empirical Study

**Version**: 1.1 (June 2026)  
**Duration**: 45–60 minutes  
**Participants**: Procurement leaders (CPO / Head of Procurement), Category Managers, CFOs with procurement oversight, Internal Audit / Compliance leads. Mix of public sector, large private, and mid-sized companies.  
**Language**: Polish (primary) + English version available.  
**Recording**: Audio (with consent) + detailed notes. Transcribe key passages only. Anonymize all data.  
**Consent**: Written or recorded verbal consent including permission for anonymized use in academic research and the replication package.

---

## Interview Structure

### 1. Opening (5–7 min) – Procurement Philosophy & Context

Goal: Establish how the organization currently thinks about policy vs. procedure and the 2×2 context (Direct/Indirect × Upstream/Downstream).

Questions:
1. How does your organization currently define and document "procurement policy" versus "procurement procedure"? Can you give a concrete example of something that is policy (non-negotiable) versus something that is procedural choice?
2. When you classify purchases, do you use a distinction similar to Direct (goes into the product/service, high TCO impact) vs. Indirect (support spend)? How about strategic/Upstream (sourcing, contracting, SRM) vs. operational/Downstream (P2P execution)? How useful (or artificial) do these distinctions feel in practice?
3. In your experience, does the same purchase look very different in terms of time, effort, risk, and value creation depending on whether it is treated as a rigid formal procedure or a policy-compliant but flexible path?

### 2. High-Stakes Decisions in the 2×2 Framework (10–12 min)

Goal: Get specific, comparable stories from the four quadrants, especially Direct+Upstream (the theoretically highest-cost quadrant per the model).

Ask the respondent to pick 1–2 recent or memorable purchases and walk through them using the 2×2 lens.

Prompts:
- Tell me about a significant **Direct + Upstream** purchase (strategic sourcing for production, CAPEX, critical materials, major service contracts).
  - What was the formal procedure that "should" have been followed?
  - Did the team stay inside the procedure or use an accelerated/policy-flexible path? Why?
  - How much senior/executive and legal time was actually spent? (Try to get rough hours or % of effort.)
  - What was the real calendar time from need to contract vs. what a textbook rigid process would have taken?
  - Were there renegotiations later? Bypass or shadow processes? What positive or negative outcomes did you observe (delay, TCO, supplier performance, internal effort)?

- Repeat or contrast with one **Indirect + Downstream** purchase (standard indirect, catalog, MRP-style) and/or one from another quadrant.

Follow-ups:
- In the strategic/Direct cases, was the extra governance time (exec, legal, board) justified by better outcomes, or did it mostly protect against audit risk?
- Did digital tools (ERP, sourcing platforms) actually reduce the need for procedural checkpoints, or did the old procedures still run in parallel?

### 3. Behavioral Mechanisms & Compliance Theater (10 min)

Goal: Surface evidence for the mechanisms in the paper (accountability asymmetry, enforcement fallacy, normalization of deviance, Goodhart's Law, multitask principal-agent).

Questions:
4. When a buyer or category manager follows the formal procedure exactly but delivers a clearly suboptimal commercial result (higher price, worse TCO, longer lead time), what happens? Are they praised, ignored, or questioned?
5. Conversely, when someone achieves a much better outcome but deviates from the documented procedure (even if still within policy), how is that received by management, audit, or compliance?
6. Have you seen cases where strict enforcement of procedure drove necessary work "underground" (mail, phone, Excel, side agreements)? What were the consequences when it later became visible?
7. In your organization, is the primary KPI for the procurement function "we followed the process" or "we delivered measurable value within policy boundaries"? How does that show up in performance reviews, bonuses, or internal audits?

### 4. Technology as Compliance Infrastructure (8–10 min)

Goal: Explore the "field vs. tunnel" transition and whether modern systems are already making rigid sequential procedures obsolete.

Questions:
8. To what extent do your current systems (ERP, sourcing tools, spend analytics, approval workflows) automatically enforce policy constraints (authorization thresholds, competition requirements, documentation, conflict checks) at the transaction level, without requiring a human to "follow steps" in sequence?
9. For purchases where you have full end-to-end platform coverage, how much of the old procedural work (RFQ waiting periods, formal committee meetings, paper forms, manual compliance chasing) has genuinely disappeared vs. still runs in parallel "just in case"?
10. Have you ever deliberately relaxed or bypassed a formal procedure *because* the system now provides better real-time visibility and control than the old checkpoint process did? What was the reaction?

### 5. Conditions for Accepting More Flexibility + Closing (5–7 min)

Goal: Identify boundary conditions and willingness to experiment (important for pilot design and boundary conditions in the paper).

Questions:
11. Under what conditions would your organization (or you personally) be comfortable giving buyers and category managers significantly more procedural freedom within the same policy framework?
12. What would have to change in governance, audit approach, capability building, or technology before that felt safe?
13. If we were to run a small pilot in your organization using the ProcuraCost model (comparing actual rigid paths vs. policy-flexible alternatives on a few real purchases, with full anonymity), would you be open to participating? What would make it useful or low-risk for you?

---

## Closing & Logistics

- Thank the participant.
- Confirm consent for anonymized use.
- Ask if they are willing to be contacted for a short follow-up or to review a one-page summary of their case (optional).
- Offer to share the ProcuraCost tool + the Assumptions Explorer so they can test the 2×2 multipliers on their own scenarios.
- Collect any internal documents they are willing to share (policy docs, example process maps, TCO studies, renegotiation logs) under strict anonymity rules.

---

## Interviewer Notes & Coding Guidance

- Always tag stories with the 2×2 quadrant the respondent is describing.
- Capture rough numbers whenever possible (days, hours by role, % of effort, cost of delay, renegotiation amounts). These map directly to model parameters.
- Note emotional tone when discussing compliance pressure vs. value pressure — this supports the behavioral sections of the paper.
- After the interview, produce a short structured summary (1–2 pages) using the Pilot Case Study Protocol template.
