## Likely Concerns on 2×2 Integration

### Concern 1: "The 2×2 feels like an add-on rather than core to the model"
**Why we anticipate this**: Some readers may see the detailed 2×2 mechanics as a later refinement rather than structural.

**Sketched response**:
- Point to early introduction in 1.1 and explicit research question 2 in 1.2.
- Emphasize that the implementation in `getDimensionMultipliers` and the derive* functions was built with the 2×2 as input from the start (not retrofitted).
- Show that the largest effects are non-additive only in Direct+Upstream — this is not a minor covariate.
- Reference that the propositions in 3.8 are direct mechanical predictions of the 2×2, not generic afterthoughts.

### Concern 2: "The 2×2 claims are too strong given that the multipliers are not yet empirically calibrated"
**Why we anticipate this**: The paper is honest that the 2×2 multipliers are modeling assumptions needing future calibration.

**Sketched response**:
- Agree that the specific numbers (1.62, 1.4, 1.22/0.82, 1.85× etc.) are currently the highest-priority parameters for empirical work.
- Clarify that the *structural claim* (that the gap is materially larger in Direct+Upstream and that senior effort and TCO leverage shift) follows from the direction and interaction logic, even if the exact magnitudes are refined later.
- Note that the four core empirics (Szucs, Beuve et al., World Bank, ISM) are applied on top of the 2×2 surface, not instead of it.
- Offer to add more sensitivity language or move some magnitude discussion to the appendix if reviewers prefer.

### Concern 3: "Why not treat the 2×2 as a simple extension or robustness check instead of elevating it to first-class?"
**Why we anticipate this**: Traditional papers often add heterogeneity analyses later.

**Sketched response**:
- The 2×2 is not post-hoc heterogeneity; it was part of the model specification that drove the function design (`getDimensionMultipliers` takes spendType and processPhase as primary inputs).
- The per-step adjustments in `deriveRigidDays` / `deriveFlexibleDays` / `deriveStaffCost` only trigger for specific quadrants — this is baked into the closed forms.
- Treating it as "just an extension" would understate how the model behaves differently across real procurement contexts (strategic direct sourcing vs. transactional indirect).

## Likely Concerns on Reproducibility

### Concern 4: "The reproducibility claims are strong for a paper that is still at the model + demonstration stage"
**Why we anticipate this**: Many papers claim "transparency" without providing live exports or closed forms.

**Sketched response**:
- We are not claiming full empirical validation yet. We are claiming that the *measurement instrument itself* is fully traceable and exportable.
- Every number in the paper can be regenerated from the live tool using the exact inputs + the documented functions.
- The JSON export contains the precise multiplier vector, derived intermediates, and sources — this goes beyond what most theoretical or simulation papers provide.
- The formal statement + 3.8 recipe + replication/README subsection are there precisely so others can audit or extend without reverse-engineering.

### Concern 5: "Reproducibility infrastructure is nice but secondary to the main contribution"
**Why we anticipate this**: Some reviewers may see the exports, templates, and checklists as "extra" rather than core.

**Sketched response**:
- The methodological contribution explicitly includes treating ProcuraCost as a formal, open measurement model (see contribution #3).
- The artifacts (propositions document, survey crosswalk, interview protocol, pilot template, red lines note, feedback log, etc.) are designed to lower the barrier for the empirical validation phase that the paper itself says is still needed.
- If the reproducibility layer is seen as secondary, we are happy to move some of the technical detail to an appendix while keeping the high-level claims in the main text.

### Concern 6: "The replication package is still 'skeleton' level"
**Why we anticipate this**: The current replication folder has one detailed example + structure, not dozens of cases.

**Sketched response**:
- We agree it is currently a high-quality skeleton rather than a complete multi-case archive.
- The design choice was to first stabilize the paper + the export mechanism itself, then populate with real pilot data.
- The "Reproducing the numbers in this paper" subsection + the sample export already allow a reader to verify the specific claims made in v1.0.
- We are happy to label it clearly as "v1.0 review skeleton" and commit to expanding it with the first wave of pilot cases.

## Likely Concerns on Overall Positioning

### Concern 7: "The paper is still too model-heavy and light on empirical grounding"
**Sketched response**:
- This is fair and aligns with our own limitations section.
- The current contribution is the integrated model + 2×2 + the open measurement infrastructure that makes the next empirical step feasible and reproducible.
- We are explicitly seeking collaboration for the empirical phase (see pitch).

### Concern 8: "Some of the strong language ('first-order', 'strategic tax', 'no tunnel survives...') may be hard for reviewers to accept without more data"
**Sketched response**:
- We can soften some phrasing if it comes across as over-confident.
- However, the core analytical claim — that the cost of rigidity is materially larger in Direct+Upstream because of the simultaneous amplification across multiple dimensions — is directly visible in the current implementation and the four empirics we layer on top.
- We are open to moving the strongest wording to the abstract/conclusion or qualifying it more explicitly with "according to the current parameterization of the model."
