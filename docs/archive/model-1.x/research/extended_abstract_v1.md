# Extended Abstract

**The Hidden Cost of Procedural Compliance: A Transparent Simulation Framework for Rigid and Policy-Based Procurement**

Paweł Mamcarz  
June 2026

**Target venues:** IPSERA, EGPA, Journal of Public Procurement

---

## Abstract

Organizations can conflate procurement *policy* (binding constraints) with *procedure* (one operational path), encouraging audit safety over value-seeking judgment. We develop an explicit 2×2 simulation framework (Direct/Indirect × Upstream/Downstream) that turns this proposed mechanism into testable cost components. ProcuraCost combines one direct empirical anchor on rigidity and renegotiation with explicit assumptions for TCO, bypass, technology, process duration, and contextual interactions. Its outputs are reproducible model simulations, not empirically validated estimates of organizational effects. Szucs (2024) provides an essential boundary condition: high discretion can itself raise prices and select less productive suppliers.

---

## Problem

Procurement officers face identical policy constraints but choose radically different methods. One follows a rigid multi-step tender (180 days for a fleet purchase); another uses accelerated competitive dialogue within the same policy (45 days). Both are compliant on paper. Only the second serves organizational value. The gap is not small and is systematically larger in strategic, high-leverage spend.

Literature attributes rigidity to accountability pressures and isomorphism, yet provides limited quantification of the *opportunity costs* imposed by treating one procedure as equivalent to policy.

---

## Framework: Tunnel vs. Field + 2×2

We formalize the distinction as **Tunnel** (procedure-first: single path, human as step-executor, bypass under pressure) versus **Field** (policy-first: bounded space of compliant actions, human as value navigator, continuous compliance via modern systems).

The 2×2 dimensions (Direct vs. Indirect spend × Upstream/strategic vs. Downstream/operational phase) are structural, not covariates. They produce non-additive effects:

- Direct + Upstream: tcoMultiplier up to 1.62, delayMultiplier 1.4, +22% calendar boosts on governance steps, executive effort up to 1.85×.
- Indirect + Downstream: materially smaller gaps.

The current parameterization predicts larger gaps in high-leverage quadrants; this is a mechanical implication to be tested, not an observed result.

## The ProcuraCost Measurement Model

ProcuraCost is a deterministic candidate measurement instrument, not a black-box calculator.

Core functions:
- `getDimensionMultipliers(spendType, processPhase)` — returns the six active contextual multipliers.
- `deriveRigidDays` / `deriveFlexibleDays` — apply per-step 2×2 adjustments (1.22 / 0.82).
- `deriveStaffCost` — layers role multipliers on participation matrices.

Researcher Export exposes inputs, multipliers, both bypass probabilities, role-level staff costs, intermediate rates/probabilities, cost components, and source or assumption annotations.

Full closed forms and per-step rules are in `docs/research/model_specification_draft.md`. Model-output claims can be regenerated with `npm run replicate`.

## Results and Testable Propositions

Built-in scenario outputs vary from 18.1% to 1074.7% in model v1.2. This wide range demonstrates sensitivity to assumptions and rules out presenting a single effect-size range as an empirical result.

We derive four core propositions directly from the 2×2 mechanics (full set in `testable_propositions_v1.md`):

- **P1 (Gap amplification)**: Opportunity cost gap is materially larger for Direct×Upstream than Indirect×Downstream.
- **P2 (Senior effort concentration)**: Executive + manager + lawyer share of staff cost rises sharply in Upstream (especially Direct+Upstream).
- **P3 (Bypass moderation)**: Bypass probability is positively moderated by the Direct×Upstream cell.
- **P4 (Cost composition)**: In Direct+Upstream the marginal cost shifts from delay toward foregone TCO.

Multiple theoretical traditions motivate the hypothesis that enforcement-only responses can fail; the empirical design must test both the costs of rigidity and the governance risks of discretion.

## Implications and Next Steps

The policy/procedure distinction is potentially consequential. Modern information systems may enforce some policy boundaries continuously, but whether this safely replaces specific procedural gates is an empirical and legal question.

Immediate next steps (detailed in `docs/RESEARCH_PAPER_ACTION_PLAN.md`):
- Empirical validation via pilots and survey using the provided instruments.
- Pre-registration of core hypotheses.
- Conference submissions (IPSERA, EGPA) using this extended abstract.

The model is an instrument. The artifacts exist to lower the barrier for rigorous empirical work and collaboration.
